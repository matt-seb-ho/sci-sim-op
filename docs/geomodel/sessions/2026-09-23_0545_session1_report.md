## The findings, in six lines

1. **The model GEOS runs on is thin.** At FORGE (Fei 2025) it is one uniform granite
   block + stress, pressure and temperature gradients + fracture planes fitted to
   microseismicity. The geologic model is far richer, and the two are different artifacts.
2. **The FORGE expert model is public**, in versions: 2018 Leapfrog surfaces (1107) →
   2019 block model on a 50 m mesh with P/T/stress (**1205**, verified by opening it) →
   2022 (1397) → 2025 (1812) native-state models. There is no native Leapfrog project.
3. **Experts judge a model by matching observations, but that is not enough on its own.**
   Two different stress values fit the same test (Fei), and the WHOLESCALE model is 4× off
   on subsidence. Hence three axes: artifact vs expert, prediction on held-out data, and
   Sherman's review.
4. **A time cut gives a clean partition.** Inputs are everything published before
   2019-09 (58 submissions). Wells drilled later (16A, 56-32, 78B-32) are **blind-well
   tests** that need no GEOS runs.
5. **San Emidio's 2022 model and GEOS inputs are not public.** Also, the report names 8
   faults, not the "24" Sherman recalled, and the geologic model was built by the operator
   (Ormat). His "year" was likely spent on conversion and calibration; ask him
   ([`../TERMINOLOGY.md`](../TERMINOLOGY.md) §3).
6. **LLNL rules out Chinese models.** The current campaign models (glm, deepseek) and
   tonight's pilot model (Xiaomi mimo) are fine for research, but not as evidence of
   LLNL deployability.
